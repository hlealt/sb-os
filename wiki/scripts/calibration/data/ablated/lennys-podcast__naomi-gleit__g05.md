# Naomi Gleit — Meta's Head of Product on Zuckerberg, Early Growth, and Clarity

## Substance

- [[naomi-gleit.md]] (head of product at [[meta.md]], employee #29, longest-serving exec after Mark Zuckerberg) ran early Facebook growth via [[growth-accounting.md]] — net daily growth = new users + resurrected users (returning after inactivity) − stale users (inactive 30 days) — shifting focus from raw acquisition to retention and resurrection.[^1]
- The growth method followed the [[understand-work.md]] Understand→Identify→Execute loop: instrument and gather data, pinpoint the key lever/bottleneck, then build experiments; the team removed both macro barriers (language, device/mobile access) and micro frictions (email-confirmation steps).[^1]
- A clear shared activation metric (Facebook's "7 friends in 10 days"-style milestone that correlates with retention) gives teams a single rallying growth target — even if the exact number is imperfect.[^1]
- Her signature leadership practice is : getting all stakeholders to share the same understanding of facts, options, trade-offs, and decisions (not necessarily agreeing) so discussion focuses on real disagreements, not misunderstandings.[^1]
- She operationalizes clarity through [[canonical-everything.md]] — one canonical doc, canonical nomenclature, visuals, meeting cadences, and channels per project — plus numbered lists (so people can reference "item 2"), pre-reads 24h ahead, live-edited decisions, and a red/yellow/green traffic-light option matrix (criteria as columns).[^1]
- She frames the PM as an orchestra conductor — not the star, but the one who brings engineering/design/policy/legal into harmony — while holding the closest perspective to the CEO/founder; she values the [[disagreeable-giver.md]] (Adam Grant's likability×motivation framework) who challenges directly in the company's interest.[^1]
- Career and personal principles: persist until you get in (she did informal PM work before the title); block 2–3-hour unscheduled time with sparring partners to form a first-party perspective; "stand up into your fear" (commit decisively); and treat consistent exercise as training for life.[^1]
- Contrarian: growth is not only about growth teams — Facebook's early growth rested heavily on strong product-market fit, and growth-team optimizations are often incremental gains on an already-inevitable trend.[^1]
